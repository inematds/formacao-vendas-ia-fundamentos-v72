Lesson 1 · 15 min

Impostor syndrome: proof before speeches

You feel you can't charge for something you haven't yet seen work with your own hands. The suspicion is reasonable, which is why a pep talk doesn't fix it. What fixes it is your own proof: do the service you sell a few times, record what happened, and book a conversation.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1See selling as getting the next step
  2. 2Recognize the impostor cycle and its only way out
  3. 3Do the service you sell before you defend it
  4. 4Build your 7-day anti-impostor plan

Goal: replace the impostor feeling with your own proof: do the service you sell three times and book a conversation within 7 days.

01 · The impostor cycle and the way out

The cycle feeds itself and never produces experience. Only one arrow leads out of it. Click each stage.

Click each block in the diagram to see what it does.

Confidence in yourself doesn't come from conviction. It comes from your own historical data.

02 · Four ideas that unlock you

Each one answers a common excuse.

1. Everyone sells

Selling, here, means getting another person to accept the next step with you: replying, booking the conversation, approving a small test.

You already do this every day, you just don't call it that. If you already sell without noticing, the problem isn't aptitude: it's method.

A reading that blocks you"If the service is good, it sells itself." A good service nobody knows about doesn't bring in revenue.

2. Repetition that counts

You turn the car key without anxiety because it started the last hundred times. The confidence to say your price is built the same way: recorded repetition.

Counts: the same service in different contexts, with the time noted and another person using it. Doesn't count: redoing the test just for yourself or switching tools with every project.

3. The opposite trap

"I need to do one more" can become a permanent excuse. The "being ready" line moves a little further ahead every time you get close.

The yardstick: keep going until you can show one complete thing from start to finish. From then on, service and conversation move together.

Escape signalYou have more versions of the presentation than conversations you've had.

4. Nobody is ready

People who are already established also started without knowing how to price or where to find clients. They started and corrected along the way.

Swap "am I ready?" for "what's the smallest thing I can deliver well this week?" The second question has an answer, and it fits on a calendar.

03 · Templates to copy

Run the prompt in the AI assistant you use. The plan is saved in a file with today's date.

Prompt · proof inventory
You are my sales coach. I sell <the service: diagnosis, review,
advisory or teaching> to <type of company I want to serve>.

Here is everything I have already done or tested, unfiltered:
<one line per item, including unpaid work and tests that failed>

Do three things, in this order:
1. For each item, write the EVIDENCE in one sentence that I could say
   in a conversation with a client, without exaggerating and without making up numbers.
2. Point out the GAPS: what a buyer of this service would ask that I
   still can't answer from my own experience.
3. Propose 3 small things I can do in 7 days to close the
   most serious gaps, each with the observable result it would give me.

Be tough. If a piece of evidence is weak, say it's weak and explain why.
Anti-impostor plan · 7 days
ANTI-IMPOSTOR PLAN · start <date>

Days 1 and 2 · pick ONE service and do it once
  - service: <diagnosis / review / teaching session>
  - case: <real or fictional company, but realistic>
  - if you don't finish in 2 days, the scope was too big: cut it in half

Days 3 and 4 · redo it in 2 other contexts (different industries)
  - time for each run: ___ / ___ / ___
  - what broke: ___

Day 5 · 3 outside people use or read it, with no explanation from you
  - where they got stuck: ___

Day 6 · proof inventory (prompt above), one sentence per piece of evidence

Day 7 · one scheduled conversation
  - with whom: ___   date and time: ___

04 · Practice

Do this week. The two together break the cycle with facts, not willpower.

1 · Proof inventory

25 min

List everything you've already done related to the service you want to sell and run the inventory prompt. Save the answer in a file with today's date.

Done when: the file has at least 3 pieces of evidence, one sentence each, with no made-up numbers, and at least 2 uncomfortable gaps pointed out.

2 · 7-day plan with the conversation scheduled

20 min

Fill in the anti-impostor plan. Before starting day 1, put the day 7 conversation on your calendar with someone you already know. The goal is not to sell: it's to talk about what you did with someone who isn't you.

Done when: the day 7 conversation is on your calendar with name, date and time, and the service was done in 3 contexts with the time noted.
