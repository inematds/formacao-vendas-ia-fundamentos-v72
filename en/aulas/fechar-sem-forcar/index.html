Lesson 5 · 17 min

Closing without pushing: the smallest yes

Someone who walks into the first conversation wanting to sign a big project is asking for something the other side can't give yet: there isn't enough accumulated trust. What you close on the first call is the next step. In this lesson, the first yes is a small trust-building service, with a written scope, and each promise kept moves you up a step.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1Ask for a yes the size of the trust that exists
  2. 2Find the price where the client decides on their own, without seeming suspicious
  3. 3Protect the scope from skeletons in the closet
  4. 4Say "not now" and climb the scope ladder

Goal: propose a small first yes of diagnosis, review or teaching, with a written scope and what is left out.

01 · The scope ladder

You don't skip steps with persuasion techniques. You climb with a track record. Click on each step.

Click each block in the diagram to see what it does.

The size of the yes you can ask for is always proportional to the trust that already exists.

02 · The rules of the first yes

Each one prevents a common way of losing the client.

1. Below the decision threshold

There is an amount below which people stop deliberating. For small and mid-sized companies approached cold, that threshold tends to sit around US$ 100, as a reference only; adapt to your market.

The logic isn't about the money: discussing it internally would cost more time than the amount at stake.

Ask on the call"When you test a new vendor, how much usually goes through without turning into an approval process?"

2. Cheap, not suspicious

The first yes is cheap relative to the perceived value, never too cheap for the size of the problem: a price out of scale becomes a sign of a scam.

Free makes it worse too: with no price, the client can't calibrate what they're getting, doesn't prioritize it on their calendar, and you lose the reference point for the second request.

3. Skeletons in the closet

Every company has the spreadsheet that holds everything up, the old system, and the person who never answers. The client has lived with them for so long that they no longer even see them.

Ask to see a real screen or report before you quote, and write down in the agreement what is not included.

4. Promise less

Whoever promises the world inflates the denominator and guarantees disappointment before starting.

The test before you promise: "would I deliver this even if my week goes wrong?" If not, you're creating a debt that comes due at delivery.

Saying no builds trust"We'll do that in step two." It preserves the client's idea, protects the deadline, and creates the reason for the next conversation.

03 · Templates to copy

The prompt designs the offer. You use the phrase when the client starts dumping ideas on you.

Prompt · your first-yes offer
You are a sales strategist for people who sell trusted services
in AI (diagnose, review, advise on an ongoing basis, teach).
What I sell today: <1 sentence>
My target client: <industry, size and role of the decision maker>

Design 3 "first yes" offers, one for each service that makes
sense (short diagnostic, review, teaching session). Each with:
1. Name of the deliverable (short, no jargon)
2. What is delivered (at most 3 items) and deadline (fits in 5 business days)
3. What is NOT included
4. Illustrative price range at the threshold where the client decides alone
   (I will adapt it to my market)
5. The result they perceive (time, money or risk)
6. What this deliverable reveals to me about their business
Rank by ease of delivery x perceived value and explain in 2
lines why #1 won. Do not propose building an agent or a system.
Phrase · reduce without refusing
"I liked all of this, it can be done. Just not all at the same time.
Of the things you mentioned, which one, if it were solved
by <short deadline, e.g.: next Friday>, would already change your week?
I'll start with that one. The others come into the next conversation,
once I really know your <processes / systems / data>."

04 · Practice

Leave with an offer ready for the next conversation.

1 · First-yes offer

25 min

Run the prompt and pick offer #1. Write down in a note: name, what it delivers, what it doesn't deliver, deadline, and a price range adapted to your market.

Done when: the chosen offer passes the 3 tests: deliverable within 5 business days without access to internal systems, a price the client decides on alone, and one line saying "not included".

2 · Rehearsed "not now" phrase

10 min

Adapt the reduce-without-refusing phrase to your service and rehearse it out loud five times.

Done when: you say the phrase from memory, without apologizing, and it ends in a question that asks the client to pick just one thing.

05 · Quick quiz

4 questions. The explanation appears after your attempt.

Question 1 of 4

First conversation with a cold-approached client. What do you ask for at the end?

Question 2 of 4

Question 3 of 4

Question 4 of 4
