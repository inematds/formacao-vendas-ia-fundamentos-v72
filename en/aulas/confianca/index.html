Lesson 3 · 16 min

Trust 101: the sum of micro-sales

A stranger who stops you on the street with an offer hears "no," and the "no" isn't about the product. It's about the lack of history. Trust isn't friendliness: it's a pattern of promises kept. In this lesson you learn to build it from the very first minute, without pushing and without lying.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1See a sale as a queue of small sales
  2. 2Measure trust by the size of the answer
  3. 3Build connection without manufactured rapport
  4. 4Open the conversation with structure and a promise of an honest verdict

Goal: lead the first minutes of a conversation calmly, measure the temperature, and announce a structure that ends in an honest verdict.

01 · The queue of micro-sales

Closing a contract is just the last point of a queue that started long before. Click each step.

Click each block in the diagram to see what it does.

Look at the arrows, not the boxes: it's between one step and the next that most sales die, each for a different reason.

02 · The first minutes

Four things that support everything that comes after.

1. Measure the temperature

Ask an open question, one that can't be answered with yes or no, and measure the length of the answer.

One word ("normal") calls for more proof before you ask more. A paragraph with a question back clears you to go deeper.

Open question"How does your customer service work today?"

2. Connection without lying

Mention something true about the industry that you actually studied, or a common problem in similar companies, and ask if it happens there.

Invented coincidences and memorized compliments get noticed. From then on, everything you say is filtered as a sales technique. A small, true bond is worth more than a big, fake one.

3. The basics of the call

Five minutes before: camera framed, microphone tested, notifications silenced. Join on time, not early and not late.

Dress to match the client's industry. If they don't turn on their camera, you keep yours on and read that as low temperature.

4. Calm, not excitement

If this were your fourth meeting of the day, you'd be relaxed. The client reads that calm as "this person does this all the time."

Polite, not excited. Steady voice. When you talk about what you've been doing, mention work in progress, even if it's a pilot of your own.

03 · Templates to copy

You run the question bank once per client profile. You rehearse the opening until it becomes automatic.

Prompt · temperature question bank
Sou consultor e vendo <diagnóstico, revisão, acompanhamento ou ensino de IA>.
Meu cliente ideal é <setor e porte da empresa>.

Escreva 8 perguntas abertas para o começo de uma reunião com esse perfil.
Regras: nenhuma pode ser respondida com sim ou não; 4 leves (contexto,
rotina) e 4 que levem o cliente a descrever um problema operacional.
Para cada pergunta, escreva o que uma resposta CURTA e o que uma resposta
LONGA me dizem sobre o nível de confiança dele.
Prompt · your 60-second opening
Escreva a abertura falada de uma reunião de diagnóstico, com no máximo
60 segundos de fala.
Contexto:
- eu vendo <o serviço>
- o cliente é <setor, porte e cargo de quem estará na call>
- o contato veio de <prospecção ou indicação>

A abertura precisa, nesta ordem:
(1) cumprimentar em tom calmo, sem euforia;
(2) anunciar a estrutura em quatro blocos: contexto, perguntas, o que temos,
    veredito;
(3) avisar que no final eu digo com honestidade se posso ou não ajudar, e
    se não for comigo, quem procurar;
(4) terminar com UMA pergunta aberta que meça a temperatura.
Tom: direto, sem jargão, sem superlativo. Devolva só o texto falado.

04 · Practice

Do this before your next conversation with a client.

1 · Temperature question bank

15 min

Run the prompt for your ideal customer profile from lesson 2. Cut the questions you wouldn't ask over coffee and keep the rest in a note that stays open during the call.

Done when: you have 8 open questions, none answerable with yes or no, each with the reading of the short and the long answer.

2 · Timed 60-second opening

20 min

Run the opening prompt, adjust it to your own voice, and record yourself speaking. Watch it once with the sound off to check posture and framing, and once with the sound on to check the tone.

Done when: the opening fits in 60 seconds on the timer, announces the 4 blocks, promises the honest verdict, and ends with an open question.

05 · Quick quiz

4 questions. The explanation appears after your attempt.

Question 1 of 4

You ask "how does your customer service work today?" and you hear "normal." What does that indicate?

Question 2 of 4

The prospect never even opened your first email. Where is the problem?

Question 3 of 4

To build rapport, you say you have "a business just like" the client's, which isn't true. What's the risk?

Question 4 of 4

Why promise an honest verdict up front, even a negative one?

06 · Summary

Trust is a track recordPromises kept, not friendliness. Without a track record, the client only buys on low price.
Selling is a queueEach step fails for its own reason; fix the right one.
TemperatureThe length of the answer to an open question tells you how much trust already exists.
Calm and structureAnnounce the conversation and promise an honest verdict.
